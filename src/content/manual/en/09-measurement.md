Data replace the estimates above as soon as the programme runs. The pilot is therefore designed so that each key number can be verified statistically.

| What we verify | How | How many people are necessary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incremental conversion in the window of change | a randomised experiment: a half of the new residents and of the new employees of partner companies get the welcome offer, the other half does not (as in Copenhagen and Stuttgart) | about 700 in each group, to see a difference of 5 points (10% against 15%) with 80% confidence; about 5,000 adults move to Ostrava each year |
| Decrease of churn of regular passengers | a step-by-step launch by district and a comparison of differences in differences (districts where the programme runs against districts where it does not run yet) | a decrease of 1.5 points shows on about 9,000 holders in each group (for example Poruba against Jih); a decrease of 0.75 points needs about 35,000, which is the whole city and a comparison with history |
| Returns | a random half of those who left gets the win-back offer at once, the other half 3 months later | about 1,000 in each group |
| Redemption rate and interest | data of the partners and of ODIS | all members |

The results of the pilot are used to **update the estimates** (in the Bayesian way: the ranges in the Business model are the prior, and the data make them narrower). After 6 months, the 10-year plan is calculated again and the city decides about the expansion.

| Indicator | How | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Outflow:** the share of holders who do not renew their pass in a year | ODIS data, pilot district against control district | lower than in the control |
| **Inflow:** new holders of a long-term pass | ODIS data, randomised experiment | higher than in the control |
| **Net flow** = inflow − outflow | ODIS data | positive and better than the control |
| Retention of new holders after 6 and 12 months | ODIS data | at least 70% after a year |
| Share of new holders who drove before | a questionnaire at registration and after 6 months | to verify |
| Redemption rate of discounts | data of the partners | above 50% |
| Cost of a net passenger-year | cost ÷ extra passenger-years | below the yearly revenue from a passenger |

**Fairness rules:**

- The value of a point is public and does not change backwards. Points are valid for 12 months, with a notice before they expire.
- The prize draw is free, and tickets for it cannot be bought. No false countdowns and no "last pieces".
- No messages that punish or frighten with a loss ("You disappointed us", "you lost your streak"). An offer of a way back comes in their place. At most 2 messages in each phase, and one click to unsubscribe.
- Everything is possible without a phone: the card, the website, a DPO ticket office.
- Against cheating: daily caps, points only for paid trips, removal of points when a pass is returned, a check of unusual patterns.
- Data only with consent and only in the scope of pass purchases. No tracking of location, no sale of data, evaluation only in aggregate.

### The data dividend: what the city learns

Today the city knows the state: how many passengers DPO carried in a year. It does not know the flows: who came, who left, and why. Fajnovka is the first tool that gives this knowledge, because a member has a reason to register the card and to answer one question at the right moment.

| What the city learns | From what | What it is good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How many holders do not renew their pass, by district and by age group | renewals of passes on registered cards | to see where passengers leave, earlier than the annual report shows it |
| Why people leave | one question on day 30 after the pass ends | a reason that DPO can act on: a connection, a price, a move |
| Which offers bring people back | win-back experiments with a control group | money goes only to offers that work |
| Which events and partners move people by public transport | redemption of rewards at partners | planning of services for large events |
| What a household does when it gives up a car | members of "One car less", with consent | evidence for the parking and housing policy of the city |

The rules stay the same as above: consent, no location, no sale, and results only in aggregate. An optional application can show a member their level and rewards, but the card is enough, and the application must not collect more data than the card. The city gets better mobility analytics for its decisions (new lines, parking, the Sustainable Urban Mobility Plan) and the member gets a better service. This is a benefit that the cost model does not count.
