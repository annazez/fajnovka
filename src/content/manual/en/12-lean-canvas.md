The canvas has two columns, because Fajnovka has two customers. The city buys a shift of flows. The user "pays" with a change of habit and gets experiences and pride.

| Block | City of Ostrava (the paying customer) | User with an ODISka |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Problem** | a net outflow from public transport to cars, although public transport has free capacity (12% in the rush hour) and 258,764 people live within 5 minutes of a stop; nobody measures the flows | "The change looks like a risk without a reward." "We cannot manage the children without the second car." "I have travelled for years and nobody values it." |
| **Alternatives today** | discounts by age, parking policy, campaigns, investment in lines | the car; single tickets; the 10+2 programme; the castle discounts with an ODISka |
| **Segments** | the transport and culture departments, DPO, KODIS | families with 2+ cars; people in a window of change; drivers who commute; regular passengers |
| **First customers** | the pilot district, the head of the transport department | parents of first-year pupils, employees of the city and of 2–3 companies, new residents, Diamond members at the start |
| **Unique value** | measurable retention and return of passengers for 0.55% of the compensation, payback within 8 years, CZK +256 million for the region in 10 years | a small step, a reward at once, and a level that I am proud of |
| **Solution** | one programme for transport and culture, data about the flows, a pilot with a control district | steps, levels and the prize draw, points for change, the member life cycle, the shared account, "One car less" |
| **Channels** | LabZero, the transport department, the city council | schools, employers, the city office, Colours of Ostrava, DPO vehicles, the ODIS e-shop, ambassadors, stories of winners |
| **Flow of money** | the city budget pays (CZK 11.6 million a year), possibly with the region; the revenue of DPO from kept, returned and new passes comes back (CZK 4.0 million in year 1, a net CZK +18 million in 10 years), and so does the revenue of KODIS and of the partners | pays nothing extra; companies can buy points as a benefit |
| **Costs** | a team of 3 people in DPO, IT and automation, targeted campaigns, the prize draw, "One car less", the contribution to discounts | the time for registration and for the change of habit |
| **Key metrics** | outflow, inflow and net flow against the control; the price of a net passenger-year | the first reward within 2 weeks, steps up, retention after 12 months |
| **Unfair advantage** | the city owns DPO and the cultural institutions and can connect them | the user already has an ODISka, children get it for free, and nobody else can give a level from the city |

**Assumptions to verify, from the most risky:**

1. ODIS gives the data about purchases and the history of passes to the points account, and can connect the ODISka cards of a family.
2. Levels, the prize draw and win-back messages decrease the churn of regular passengers (without this, the programme does not pay back).
3. Partners accept 50% discounts with a contribution from the city of about 15%.
4. The welcome offer in the window of change brings at least 300 truly new passengers a year.
5. Families with two cars are willing to give up one of them for a package with a value of about CZK 4,900.
