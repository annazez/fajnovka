---
title: Ten years with Fajnovka and without it
order: 8
---

Without the programme, the revenue of DPO from long-term passes follows the long-term trend and decreases from CZK 272 million to CZK 246 million a year. In 10 years, that is CZK 148 million less than today. In the base scenario, Fajnovka slows the decrease: in 2036 the revenue is CZK 20 million higher than without it, the programme pays back in 2034, and in 10 years it brings a net CZK +18 million to DPO and the city. For the whole region, with KODIS, the partners and fewer cars, the benefit is about CZK +256 million. The full calculation is in the financial model of the team (Finance Canvas, sheet 8, "10-year plan").

| Assumption | Pessimistic (P10) | Base (P50) | Optimistic (P90) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Truly new passengers a year | 280, from year 3 less by 10% each year | 410 | 600 |
| Decrease of churn of regular passengers | 0 | 0.75 points (370 people a year) | 1.5 points (735 people) |
| Returns of those who left, a year | 360 | 540 | 790 |
| Extra regional commuters (KODIS) | 100 | 200 | 350 |
| Operating cost a year (without depreciation) | CZK 11.5 million (+10%) | CZK 10.4 million | CZK 10.4 million |
| Investment | CZK 4.5 million in 2027, renewal of the IT for CZK 2 million in 2031 and 2035 | the same | the same |

Shared assumptions: a start in 2027 with 70% of the effect; without the programme, the revenue decreases by 1% a year, like the number of passengers in 2010–2025; of the new and returned passengers, 70% stay for the first year and then 85% each year; of the kept regular passengers, 85% each year; prices of 2025; a discount rate of 4%.

![Revenue of DPO from long-term passes with Fajnovka and without it, base scenario, CZK million. Rough estimate. The starting revenue is from the DPO annual report 2025.](figure:revenue)

The difference adds up in time: each year brings a new cohort, and a part of the earlier cohorts still rides. In 2036, about 6,200 extra people ride because of Fajnovka in the base scenario.

![Extra passengers because of Fajnovka in each year, by scenario, 2027–2036. Rough estimate.](figure:passengers-extra)

The first years are the most expensive (the investment, and the effect only starts). The base scenario pays back in 2034, the optimistic scenario in 2030, and the pessimistic scenario does not pay back by 2036.

![Cumulative net benefit for DPO and the city, by scenario, CZK million, prices of 2025. The line crosses zero in the year of payback. Rough estimate.](figure:payback)

The benefit divides into about three equal parts between the new, the returned and the kept passengers. But returns and retention cost a fraction of acquisition.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Extra revenue for DPO | 53 | 131 | 215 |
| Cost of the programme | 123 | 113 | 113 |
| **Net benefit for DPO and the city** | **−70** | **+18** | **+102** |
| Net present value (4%) | −59 | +9 | +74 |
| Payback | not by 2036 | 2034 | 2030 |
| Extra passengers in 2036 | 2,400 | 6,200 | 10,200 |

### Wider benefits: KODIS, partners and fewer cars

The DPO model counts only the revenue of DPO. But Fajnovka brings money to other places too:

- **KODIS and the regional carriers:** commuters from the region who come through park and ride and company challenges buy regional ODIS passes. An estimate of 200 people a year × CZK 7,000 (to verify with KODIS) gives about CZK 42 million in 10 years. A part of it returns to DPO through the division of revenue.
- **Partners:** the customers pay the other half of the discount and spend something extra (refreshments, a companion). With CZK 2.7 million of discounts a year, this is about CZK 3.5 million of revenue a year, or CZK 34 million in 10 years. About a half goes to city institutions (the zoo, theatres, swimming pools), so their subsidy from the city decreases too.
- **Fewer cars:** each passenger who would otherwise drive saves about 3,000 passenger-kilometres by car a year. The European handbook on external costs says that cars cause on average [12 eurocents of external costs (accidents, emissions, congestion) per passenger-kilometre](https://www.infras.ch/en/projects/eu-just-under-one-billion-euros-external-environmental-accident-and-congestion-costs/). For Ostrava we count a conservative CZK 2. In 10 years, that is about CZK 162 million.
- **Households in "One car less"** save the cost of a second car (tens of thousands of crowns a year). This is a benefit for people, not for public budgets, so we do not add it.

| Net benefit for DPO and the city | −70 | +18 | +102 |
| Extra revenue of KODIS | +21 | +42 | +74 |
| Extra income of partners | +34 | +34 | +34 |
| Saved external costs of cars | +72 | +162 | +257 |
| **Total benefit for the region** | **+56** | **+256** | **+468** |

- **Extra revenue decreases the compensation** that the city pays to DPO. The net benefit of DPO is a saving for the city budget.
- **The pessimistic scenario is also positive for the region,** but not for the city budget. The region and KODIS are therefore natural partners for the financing.
- **Stop rule:** if neither the pilot nor the first 2 years of city-wide operation show a decrease of churn, and fewer than 300 truly new passengers come each year, the programme narrows to its cheapest levers (returns and levels) and the paid adverts stop. The loss then stops at about CZK −25 million.
- **Simplifications:** constant prices of 2025; the effect in the base and optimistic scenarios does not fade; new passengers ride in free capacity.
