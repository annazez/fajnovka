A city-wide Fajnovka in the integrated version (a team inside DPO, automation) costs **CZK 11.6 million a year**. That is 0.55% of the compensation that the city pays to DPO. In the first year, about CZK 4.0 million comes back in fares. Over the whole time that the passengers won in one year continue to ride, it is about CZK 19.7 million. The programme therefore stands mainly on keeping regular passengers and on the return of those who left. It brings fewer new passengers from cars than we first estimated. All numbers are a rough estimate, with the exception of the table of input data.

DPO already has a loyalty programme, 10+2. Fajnovka must absorb it, not compete with it. Its cost today (the lost revenue for the free coupons) is therefore a possible source of money.

### How many people

**Holders of a long-term pass: about 85,000 different people.** With an average price of CZK 3,400–3,800 for the annual pass and CZK 400–500 for the 30-day pass (a mix of full and reduced fares), the 162 thousand passes give 58–69 thousand annual passes and 93–104 thousand 30-day passes. An estimated 20–30 thousand different people buy the 30-day passes. Together that is 80–95 thousand people.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regular holders who pay | 85,000 | 47% | 40,000 |
| Seniors 65+ with a pass for CZK 0 | 30,000 | 30% | 9,000 |
| Occasional passengers (bank card, e-wallet) | 100,000 | 25% | 25,000 |

| **Total** |  |  | **80,410** |

**A check:** 410 new, 370 kept and 540 returned passengers make about 1,300 passengers. At about 500 trips a year each, that is 0.65 million trips, or +0.7% of all passengers carried. This is less than the growth between 2024 and 2025 (+1.5%), so the estimate is not too high.

### How many are truly new: how we estimated it

The least certain number in the model is how many people start to use public transport only because of Fajnovka. We estimated it in two independent ways, and the two results are similar: **about 410 people a year, in a range of 280–600** (the 10th and 90th percentiles). The first estimate of 2,400 was about six times higher and had no support.

**1. Bottom-up (a Monte Carlo simulation, 200,000 runs).** Each input has a range, not one number:

| Step | Range (most probable) | Support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People in a window of change each year (moved in, moved in the city, new job, parents of first-year pupils, without overlaps) | 21–27 thousand (24 thousand) | Czech Statistical Office 2025: about 6,500 moved in; the rest is an estimate |
| Of these, drive a car | 40–60% (50%) | estimate |
| The programme reaches them (city office, employers, schools) | 30–70% (50%) | estimate |
| Incremental conversion to a long-term pass | 2–10% (5%) | Copenhagen: a free month doubled the trips by public transport, but [only for people soon after a move or a change of job](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/trapol/v16y2009i6p335-343.html) (Thøgersen 2009); Stuttgart: new residents with a free pass [used public transport much more](https://forschung.uni-giessen.de/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1130366939?lang=en_GB) (Bamberg 2006) |
| Drivers outside a window of change | 60–100 thousand, 15–40% reached, conversion 0.1–0.6% | Copenhagen: for usual drivers, [no difference from the control group after 4 months](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/2294) (Thøgersen and Møller 2008) |
| Students | 5–7 thousand, conversion 0.5–3% | most of them already use public transport |

The result: the window of change brings about 330 people, other drivers 70, and students 10. In total 280 / 410 / 600 (P10 / P50 / P90).

**2. Through price elasticity.** For a person, the rewards of a newcomer have a value of about 3–8% of the price of the pass (the person must pay the other half of a discount, and does not use all discounts). A British synthesis puts the price elasticity of demand for public transport at [−0.4 in the short term and up to −1.0 in the long term](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/2034/1) (Paulley et al. 2006). For about 12,000 new holders a year, this gives 280 / 410 / 590 people. This way measures only the price effect, not the timing and the attention, so it is more a lower limit.

**3. Returns and retention in the same way.** Returns: 12,000 people who left × 30–50% who agreed to contact × 5–20% of extra returns = **540 a year (360–790)**. The success rate of win-back campaigns in the industry is [10–30%](https://opensend.com/post/win-back-campaign-success-rate-statistics), and research shows that [a win-back offer pays](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/07/150707134215.htm) (Kumar et al. 2015). Retention: a meta-analysis of 429 results from 40 years confirms that [loyalty programmes increase loyalty](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/40-years-of-loyalty-programs-how-effective-are-they-generalizati/19645230) (Belli et al. 2022), but the effects are small. A decrease of churn by 0.75 points (from 15% to 14.25%) is therefore the middle estimate, in a range of 0–1.5 points.

| --- | --- |

### Points economy

People do not use all points and discounts. A report by Bond Brand Loyalty says that [almost 30% of points stay unused each year](https://www.leat.com/resources/insights/loyalty-program-breakage). For Fajnovka, we count with 70% of points exchanged for a discount and 65% of discounts really used (the person must pay the other half). In total that is **45%** (range 30–60%). People collect 85% of the prizes from the draw.

| Who and for what | People | Points per person a year | Points issued | Discount at partners | Cost for the city |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New members: registration and steps (max. 700) | 6,410 | 530 | 3.40 million | CZK 0.77 million | CZK 0.12 million |
| Window of change (double points for 3 months) | 1,280 | 150 extra | 0.19 million | CZK 0.04 million | CZK 0.01 million |
| Occasional passengers: off-peak trips and events | 25,000 | 180 | 4.50 million | CZK 1.02 million | CZK 0.15 million |
| Park and ride with a public transport trip | 800 | 2,000 | 1.60 million | CZK 0.36 million | CZK 0.06 million |
| Regular passengers and seniors: a trip to a partner event | 49,000 | 40 | 1.96 million | CZK 0.45 million | CZK 0.07 million |
| Team challenges | 12 challenges | 5,000 per challenge | 0.06 million | CZK 0.01 million | CZK 0.00 million |
| **Total** |  |  | **11.7 million** | **CZK 2.7 million** | **CZK 0.40 million** |

The calculation: points issued × 70% × 65% × CZK 0.5 = the discount at partners. The city pays 15% of it. The partners pay 85% as the price for customers who pay the other half. Points cost the city less than 4% of the cost of the programme. With the discounts at the levels, the Fajnovka passport and the company points, it is about CZK 0.8 million a year. The redemption rate therefore changes the total cost only a little (CZK 11.3 million at 30%, CZK 12.6 million at 100%). Its main use is as an **indicator of interest**: a rate below 30% means that the rewards do not attract people.

### How to decrease the cost: integration into DPO and automation

In the first version, the largest item was a separate team of 7 people (CZK 5.5 million, a third of the cost). But a machine can do most of this work, or DPO already does it today:

| Activity | First version | Integrated version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Points, levels, notices, win-back messages | manual work in the CRM | automatic, from ODIS data (rules that are set once) |
| Prize draw | manual | automatic, with an algorithm certified by a notary |
| Partners | a manager calls them | a self-service portal: the partner enters the offer and sees how many people use it |
| Customer support | 2 own people | the DPO customer centre (50% of one job) and answers on the website |
| Analyses | own analyst | the DPO data team (25% of the cost) |
| Adverts | CZK 3.0 million of paid campaigns | CZK 1.5 million; the vehicles, the stops and the e-shop of DPO are free |
| Badges | by post to all | by post from the Silver level up, the Bronze level digitally |
| "One car less" | 200 households from the start | 100 households, more when the interest shows |

Three own people stay: the head of the programme, the partner manager, and a specialist for marketing and CRM automation. The team then costs CZK 3.2 million and not CZK 5.5 million, and the total cost decreases from CZK 16.2 million to CZK 11.6 million a year. Automation adds CZK 0.3 million of investment and CZK 0.2 million a year of operation.

### How much to put where

The rule: put each crown where a **net passenger-year** is cheapest. A net passenger-year is a year of travel that would not exist without the programme.

| Lever                      | Budget           | Net passenger-years | Price of a passenger-year |
| -------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Win-back of those who left | CZK 0.17 million | 540 × 3.3 = 1,780   | **CZK 95**                |
| Loyalty                    | CZK 1.27 million | 370 × 6.7 = 2,480   | **CZK 510**               |
| Acquisition from cars      | CZK 3.01 million | 410 × 3.3 = 1,350   | **CZK 2,220**             |

![The price of one net passenger-year by lever, in CZK. Rough estimate.](figure:levers)

- **Fajnovka is mainly a programme of retention and return.** Win-back and loyalty bring a passenger-year for CZK 95 and CZK 510, acquisition for CZK 2,220. Acquisition still pays (a passenger brings CZK 3,300 a year), but only through cheap channels in the window of change.
- **Wide adverts for drivers do not pay.** Drivers outside a window of change are only about 70 of the 410 new passengers. The money belongs to the city office, employers and schools.
- **Loyalty costs 0.47% of the revenue from long-term passes.** That is below the rule of the industry of 1–3% ([supermarkets 1–1.5%](https://enable3.io/blog/cost-of-a-loyalty-program)).
- **Churn decides.** If the churn of regular passengers does not change, the programme does not pay back even over the whole time the passengers ride (see the sensitivity table). For this reason, churn is the first thing that the pilot measures.

| View | Result |

**Sensitivity** (CZK million, year 1 / the cohort over the whole time it rides; returns are always 540):

| Truly new a year | Churn without change | Churn −0.75 points | Churn −1.5 points |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------- |
| 280              | −9.2 / −1.8          | −8.0 / +6.7        | −6.7 / +15.0      |
| 410              | −8.8 / −0.3          | −7.5 / +8.1        | −6.3 / +16.4      |
| 600              | −8.2 / +1.7          | −6.9 / +10.2       | −5.7 / +18.5      |

The number of new passengers changes the result by a few million. Churn changes it by tens of millions. A cohort pays back with only a small decrease of churn (about 15 people kept each year). Without it, the cohort does not pay back.
