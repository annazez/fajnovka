title: How Fajnovka reaches people
order: 6
---

The strategy in one sentence: speak about what the customer gets, not about what the customer must give up, and speak at the moment when the customer decides again. Each message has one small step and a reward that the customer sees at once.

Ostrava loses about 1% of its passengers each year. A loss this slow does not need one large project. It needs many small steps in the other direction. That is the motto of the programme: **small steps, big changes.**

**The sale stands on three motives, in this order:** a gain at once (points and the first reward within 2 weeks, like a coupon in a supermarket), progress (the steps and a circle that fills, like an airline status) and pride (the level and the prize draw for members of ten years, like a blood donor medal). The gain brings people in, progress keeps them, and pride changes their identity. With each step, the communication moves from "you get" to "you are".

**Positioning:** for the people of Ostrava who drive from habit, Fajnovka is a city programme on the ODISka card that changes each step towards public transport into an experience in the city. A fare discount rewards the usual trip. Fajnovka rewards change and loyalty.

**Slogan:** "Ride well. Live Ostrava."

| Group | What troubles them | Message | Call to action | Channel and moment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Family with 2 cars | the second car costs money and time | "A second car, or a family season at the zoo? Do the sums with us." | calculate the cost of the car, open a shared account | school in September; roadworthiness test and service |
| Driver who commutes | the change looks like a risk | "A month without the keys. If you do not like it, you lose nothing." | buy a 30-day pass, 250 points at once | the employer and a company challenge; January |
| Newcomer to the city | does not know the lines or the city | "Welcome to Ostrava. In the first month, the city shows itself to you." | a welcome package with double points | the city office at registration, the employer |
| Regular passenger | nobody sees their loyalty | "You have travelled with us for 15 years. Here is your Diamond level." | register the ODISka | a letter or an e-mail at the launch |
| Student | a car after school as a symbol of adult life | "To Colours by tram. Points for the next year of study." | a festival ticket on the ODISka | Colours of Ostrava, the faculties |

**Rules of communication:**

- Speak about time, money and experiences. Emissions come second.
- Never moralise to drivers.
- Give exact numbers, not adjectives ("250 points at once").
- Use the identity of Ostrava (_fajne_, _Fajnovák_).
- After each gain of points, say how many points remain to the next reward.
- Do not promise speed where public transport is slower.

**Channels:** own (the vehicles and stops of DPO, the ODIS e-shop, the ticket offices), partner (schools, employers, the city office, Colours of Ostrava, the ticket offices of theatres, the zoo and the swimming pools), paid (targeted adverts in the parts of the city that have good public transport and many cars) and earned (stories of winners and of families in "One car less" in the local media).

**Order of the launch:** first the regular passengers (a level from their history, so the programme has thousands of members and social proof at once), then the window of change (schools in September, employers and the city office all year), then the wide campaigns.

### Through people they trust

An advert tells people that public transport is good. A person they know shows it. Research confirms the power of social incentives for green behaviour (see Why it will work). Fajnovka therefore reaches people through three groups that they trust: their employer, the people they live with, and the people they admire. All three are a proposal for the pilot.

**Employers.** A job is the most frequent reason for a daily trip, and a new job is a window of change. An employer can give Fajnovka as a benefit in three ways:

- **A welcome for new employees:** double points for the steps for 3 months, with a confirmation from the employer.
- **Points as a benefit:** the employer buys points for its people, in the same way as it buys meal or leisure vouchers today. The money goes to the programme.
- **The team challenge of the month:** a ranking of companies, schools and districts, with a bonus for the team that wins.

We propose that the city starts with itself. The City Hall, the city districts and the city organisations are among the largest employers in Ostrava, and the city can decide for them without a negotiation. If the employees of the city ride with Fajnovka first, the city tests the benefit on its own people, gets the first data, and has an honest answer for each company that asks: "Do you use it yourselves?"

**Groups.** A family or a group of friends connects its ODISka cards in one account (see The reward system). The message goes from one member of the group to the next, and no advert is necessary.

**Ambassadors.** In many cities, public transport is a choice that people are proud of. In Vienna, [more people have an annual pass or another season ticket than a car](https://www.wienerlinien.at/en/web/wl-en/news/wiener-linien-reports-results-for-2025), and Wiener Linien counts 1.3 million regular customers. In New York, buses were one of the main subjects of the election for mayor in 2025: the winner, Zohran Mamdani, promised buses that are "fast and free". [The fares are still there](https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/04/137311-nyc-mayor-walks-back-free-buses-promise), so the example is not about the price. It shows that public transport can be a subject for which well-known people stand up in public.

Ostrava can do the same on a small scale. Well-known people of the city (athletes from the city clubs, actors, musicians, teachers and doctors that a district knows) show their level and say why they ride. The rules:

- An ambassador must really ride. The level on the card is the proof, and it cannot be bought.
- No fee for the ambassador. The reward is the same recognition that each member gets.
- The mayor and the councillors can be the first. A Diamond level on the card of a politician is a stronger message than a poster.
- The programme does not name ambassadors before they agree. This document names nobody.

### A customer journey: the Novák family

1. **Trigger:** in September, the daughter gets her first ODISka, and the school gives out a leaflet with the class challenge.
2. **Decision:** on the website, the family calculates the cost of the second car, opens a shared account and gets 100 points.
3. **First step:** Jana buys a trial month (+150) and, as the second adult, gets the double family bonus in September (+600).
4. **First reward:** within 2 weeks, the family goes to the zoo for half the admission.
5. **The large decision:** when the second car needs an expensive roadworthiness test, the family uses "One car less".
6. **Habit:** after a year, Jana has the Bronze level and the class leads the school ranking. Points are no longer the main reason.
