The problem of the city is not only the low share of public transport. It is the flow between the modes. People move in both directions, and mobility biographies from Cape Town showed [a long-term net shift from public to private transport](https://open.uct.ac.za/bitstream/11427/31369/4/thesis_ebe_2019_adjei_eric.pdf). The share of public transport is a state that two flows change. Fajnovka acts at the moments when people decide.

| Flow | Who | When it happens | How Fajnovka changes it | How to measure it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outflow: from public transport to the car | regular passengers, students | a child, a new job, the end of studies, a move | level, member life cycle, shared account | share of long-term passes that are not renewed in a year |
| Inflow: from the car to public transport | drivers, newcomers, parents | a new job, a move, school, a road closure | steps of passes, points for change, campaigns | new pass holders and their retention after 12 months |
| A car leaves the household | families with 2+ cars | an expensive repair or roadworthiness test, a change of job | "One car less" package | vehicle register |

Today we know only the state: the number of passengers fell from 106.3 million (2010) to 91.2 million (2025). That is about 1% each year.

![Passengers carried by DPO each year, 2010–2025, in millions. Source: DPO annual reports.](figure:passengers)

We do not know the flows. The first data request to KODIS is therefore: how many holders of a long-term pass do not renew it each year, and how many new holders come.

Public transport has spare capacity. In the Friday afternoon rush hour (13 November 2026, 16:38, DPO API), about 12% of the technical capacity of the vehicles was in use, and 258,764 residents live within a 5-minute walk of a stop ([MAPPA](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/34a6b27c62464bcc8b03a1eae85c05c2/)). The barrier is not distance. It is habit.

The system itself is good. In 2024, [a PwC study of smart mobility](https://www.pwc.com/ee/en/publications/CEE_Smart_Cities_Mobility_Index.pdf) compared 25 cities in Central and Eastern Europe and put Ostrava in first place. The same study names the weak point: the air in Ostrava is among the most polluted in the region. A city with the best transport system in the region still loses passengers. That is why the answer is not a new line or a new vehicle, but small steps that change habits.

**Who is who:**

|  | City of Ostrava | User with an ODISka | DPO | Partners (culture, sport, businesses) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Role | paying customer, author of the challenge | customer whose behaviour we change | partner and operator | supplier of rewards |
| Wants | a better net flow, fewer cars, used capacity | a calm and cheap trip, experiences, recognition | revenue, fuller vehicles, data | new paying customers |
| Pays with | the budget | a change of habit | operation and data | a discount (it carries most of it) |
| Gets | a measurable shift of flows for a known price | a small step, a reward at once, pride | new and retained pass holders | a customer who pays the other half of the price |

**The public interest stands on three rules:** public money goes mainly to change, not to usual behaviour; rewards are mainly in the city and from free capacity; data are not sold and are not used for anything else.

**What Fajnovka does not solve:** people for whom public transport is objectively worse (slower, with changes, unreliable). That is a task for the DPO service. Fajnovka aims at people for whom public transport is comparable and whom only habit holds back, or a fear of commitment, or the feeling that nobody cares about their loyalty.

### The main competitor is the car

Fajnovka does not compete with other tickets. It competes with the car. Each new car in a household makes the next trip by car cheaper and the next trip by tram less likely. The aim is therefore that the number of cars does not grow.

A loyalty programme is the positive half of the answer. The other half is the price of parking. MAPPA shows 82,269 parking places in the city and only 706 of them are marked as paid. In [the fajnOVA survey of 2023](https://fajnova.cz/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Vysledky-dotaznikoveho-setreni-spokojenosti-s-zivotem-v-Ostrave_2023_fajnOVA.pdf) (5,190 respondents), only 31% of people were satisfied with parking, against 66% for public transport. Parking is where drivers feel pain. More paid parking places, step by step and with the money returned to public transport, make the offer of Fajnovka stronger. The decision belongs to the city. The programme works without it, but the two measures support each other.
